# Research methodology and next work

## Legal procedures ≠ Git permissions

- Article 134 identifies entitled subjects of constitutional initiative, including groups meeting the one-fifth threshold.
- Article 135 protects Chapters 1, 2, and 9 from revision by the Federal Assembly. If a proposal to revise them receives three-fifths support of the total membership of the Federation Council and State Duma, the constitutional text provides for convening a Constitutional Assembly under a federal constitutional law. The Assembly may confirm the existing Constitution or draft a new one, adopted by two-thirds of its members or submitted to nationwide voting; the nationwide route has its own turnout and approval requirements. A referendum is not mandatory in every route.
- Article 136 governs amendments to Chapters 3–8, with regional ratification in addition to the federal procedure.
- Article 137 governs changes to the Article 65 list. Name changes must not be confused with substantive Chapter 3 amendments.
- The 2020 law added a special review/voting sequence. It is not modeled as the ordinary Article 136 sequence alone.

No executable constitutional-review workflow or real institutional CODEOWNERS permissions are claimed. A Git merge is only a visualization of an enacted text change, not a model validating its legality.

## Reproduction boundaries

Raw transcriptions and Git snapshots are preserved, and the viewer is built from stored PR metadata. This prototype does not include a fully source-to-Git reproducible legal parser; human reconstruction choices remain part of the dataset. Metadata generated in 2026 is committed after the synthetic legal replay, not presented as historical evidence.
